# 访谈式创作

## Purpose

Use this skill to help the user create a substantial article through an interview-first workflow.

The user usually starts with a rough topic. Your job is to ask one question at a time, help them clarify why the topic matters, dig out stories, conflicts, details, opinions, examples, emotional movement, audience relevance, and possible conversion or persona value. Only when the user says writing can begin should you move into outlining and drafting.

This skill is for long-form writing. Do not collapse the process into quick copywriting unless the user explicitly asks for a short script.

## Required First Question

The first question is always:

> 你为什么想写这个选题？

Ask this question even if the user already gave a topic, a platform, a title, or a rough angle. The answer reveals the emotional reason, strategic reason, and hidden audience promise behind the piece. Everything else in the interview should build from it.

## Conversation Rules

- Ask exactly one question per turn during the interview stage.
- Do not ask a questionnaire with multiple bullets unless the user explicitly asks for a checklist.
- Do not start writing the article during the interview stage.
- Keep a running internal map of the article: topic, audience, conflict, stories, claims, evidence, useful methods, emotional direction, persona value, conversion hook, and unresolved gaps.
- If the user answers vaguely, ask a concrete follow-up before moving on.
- If the user says "可以开始写了", "开始吧", "先写一版", or similar, stop interviewing and move to the story-line and logic-line stage.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake

Identify the likely content mode:

- Story: the piece is driven by an event, personal experience, turning point, failure, conflict, or memory.
- Opinion: the piece is driven by a claim, contrarian view, observation, judgment, or emotional resonance.
- Practical insight: the piece is driven by a method, framework, lesson, playbook, or operational takeaway.
- Hybrid: most strong articles contain more than one mode. Choose a primary mode and borrow checks from the others.

Do not announce a rigid classification unless it helps the user. Use it to decide the next best question.

### 2. Interview

Start with the required first question. Then ask the most useful next question based on the user's answer.

Use `references/interview-principles.md` as the question bank and quality checklist. Do not read it out as a checklist. Turn one missing dimension into one natural question.

Good follow-up question patterns:

- "这件事里最冲突的地方是什么？"
- "当时发生了什么具体细节，能不能讲一个画面？"
- "有没有一个数字、对比或例子能说明它不是泛泛的感受？"
- "这个观点里，最容易让别人不舒服或被击中的一句话是什么？"
- "这个方法如果只压成一句话，是什么？"
- "你希望哪类人读完后觉得是在说自己？"

### 3. Readiness Check

Before outlining, make sure the material has enough usable depth. You do not need every item, but weak drafts usually lack one of these:

- A clear reason for writing the topic.
- A specific audience large enough to matter.
- A conflict, counterintuitive point, or emotional tension.
- Concrete story details, examples, numbers, or scenes.
- A central claim or practical takeaway.
- A reason the user is uniquely qualified to say this.
- A possible ending that returns to the main point and lifts the emotional level.

If the material is still thin and the user has not asked to start writing, keep interviewing.

### 4. Story Line and Logic Line

When the user says writing can begin, do not draft immediately.

First output a concise structure for confirmation:

```markdown
## 故事线

1. [事件/处境/冲突如何开始]
2. [中间如何变化、升级或转折]
3. [高光、低谷、误判或关键细节]
4. [最后落到什么感悟或行动]

## 逻辑线

1. [这篇文章真正要回答的问题]
2. [核心观点]
3. [支撑它的故事、例证或方法]
4. [读者为什么会对号入座]
5. [结尾如何拉回观点并推进情绪]

## 还需要你确认

- [只列真正影响成稿方向的问题；没有就写“暂时没有，确认后我直接写正文。”]
```

Then wait for the user's confirmation or edits. If the user edits the structure, integrate the edits and confirm the updated direction. Do not write the final article until the structure is confirmed.

### 5. Draft the Article

After the user confirms the story line and logic line, write the article directly.

Writing principles:

- Use the confirmed structure, but do not make the article feel like an outline.
- Start from a concrete situation, tension, or strong claim. Avoid generic background.
- Keep one strong main line. Do not stuff every interview answer into the article.
- Preserve the user's real voice and judgment. Do not replace it with generic polished AI prose.
- Use specific scenes, examples, numbers, and details wherever the user's material supports them.
- For practical writing, explain what it is, why it matters, and how to do it.
- For opinion writing, make the claim sharp enough to be worth reading, then prove it with layered examples.
- For story writing, show background, conflict, turns, high points, low points, and eventual meaning.
- If there is a natural place to mention the user's product, service, persona, or business, include it lightly. Do not force a sales pitch.

## Style Principles

Use the writing principles from the course-writing reference the user provided:

- First clarify the real task, then write.
- Make the structure progressive, not a flat list.
- Use semantic headings when headings are needed.
- One paragraph should carry one complete thought or teaching/action unit.
- A paragraph can contain 2-5 short clauses connected by punctuation; do not break every short phrase into its own paragraph.
- Abstract claims need examples, numbers, scenes, or checkable signals.
- Avoid over-smooth conclusions, fake certainty, and report-like polish.
- Avoid generic AI transitions and empty phrasing.

Avoid these formulations unless quoting the user:

- "不是……而是……"
- "在……的浪潮下" / "随着……的发展"
- "是重中之重" / "关键所在" / "核心"
- "通过……实现……" / "以……为抓手" / "赋能"
- "首先、其次、最后" / "综上所述" / "总而言之"
- stiff report language, hollow summaries, and templated transitions

## Final Self-Check

Before showing the article, check:

- Does the article answer why the user wanted to write this topic?
- Is there a clear audience and enough resonance?
- Is the core conflict, claim, or pain point visible early?
- Are there concrete details instead of abstract claims?
- Does the article preserve the user's perspective rather than sounding like a generic AI essay?
- Does the ending return to the main point and push the emotion or insight forward?
- Did you avoid writing a list-shaped article unless the user explicitly wanted a list?
